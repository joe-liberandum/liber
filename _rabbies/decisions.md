## 2026-09-17 | The Appointed Organiser is recorded as a role, not a fifth persona

**Decision.** The private tour buyer pattern found in the September 2026 research is recorded as
R1 The Appointed Organiser, a role layered over the existing four personas, in `personas.md`. No
fifth persona created. The earlier entry filing private tour buyers as a need state is revisited
rather than replaced, with its exclusion upheld and its label corrected.

**Reasoning.** The four personas separate on shopping behaviour: planning horizon, prior
relationship, trip role. This pattern cuts across all three. In the sample it appeared on a 12 month
planner and on repeat customers alike, and participants mapped to two personas at once rather than
forming a fifth cluster. Adding it to the persona set would have broken the set's one method, which
is that it was derived inductively on a single axis family. Layering it keeps both usable. It is
also not a need state, because a need state describes what someone wants on a trip and this
describes a job they have been given.

**Reversed by.** A second wave showing organisers find Rabbie's differently as well as deciding and
valuing differently. Divergence on decision pattern alone is what a role looks like; divergence on
discovery as well would make it a persona. Also reversed if the role turns out to be confined to
private tours, which would make it a product attribute rather than a customer one. That is logged
as `assumptions.md` A14.

## 2026-09-18 | Four of the eight private tour service changes are held against the margin question

**Decision.** In `strategy/2026-09_private-tours-service-model.md`, the triage rule, route knowledge
in the proposal, and the planning call are not recommended for implementation yet. They are either
held or run as a scoped trial on multi-day enquiries with hours recorded. The four items that add no
hours proceed now.

**Reasoning.** All three add consultant or driver hours to the pre-sale phase, and there is no
comparison anywhere in the project between the margin on a private tour and the margin on filled
seats on a scheduled departure. Without it the programme could be a margin loss presented as a
growth win and nobody could tell from the numbers currently held. Logged as `assumptions.md` A16.
A participant raised the same cost question himself during interview.

**Reversed by.** The margin comparison coming back in favour of private tours, which releases all
three. Or the scoped trial producing an hours figure that makes the cost tolerable regardless. Note
that a comparison coming back against private tours does not falsify any of the eight outcomes. It
would mean fixing the service for customers already buying it rather than growing the volume.

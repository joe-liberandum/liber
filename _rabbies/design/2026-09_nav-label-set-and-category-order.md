# Nav label set and category order

**Initiative.** Navigation redesign, customer mental model. Priority 1, RICE rank 6.
**Type.** Test after launch.
**Status.** Due now. Nav delivery date was 31 Aug 2026 and the initiative is in QA.

---

**Hypothesis.** Restructuring the primary navigation around customer planning dimensions (Departure City, Destinations, Duration, Interests, Most Popular) rather than Rabbie's internal categories will move more visitors into the navigation and into converting discovery paths.

**Baseline.** 74% of homepage visitors use neither navigation nor search. Search converts at 23.98%, navigation at 18.73%.

**Variant.** The shipped navigation. The test is on label set and category order within it, not on whether to ship.

**Primary metric.** Share of homepage visitors engaging with navigation. Secondary: navigation-originated session conversion rate, measured against the 18.73% baseline.

**Falsified by.** Navigation engagement staying flat at roughly 26% of homepage visitors, or navigation-originated conversion failing to close the gap with search.

**Decision it unblocks.** Whether the customer-intent taxonomy holds, which the IA restructure and the landing page templates both build on. If the labels do not move engagement, the assumption underneath the whole wayfinding programme is weaker than assumed.

**Constraint.** Salience is making SEO changes on the same URLs. Coordinate before scheduling, and separate the read from any SEO change landing in the same window.

**Open.** Two business questions were folded into the nav work: late availability link placement and sale placement. Both should be isolated in the test rather than shipped as settled.

# 1. Executive Summary

*Slide framing text: "Key findings from the customer and booking database (2012–2026), combining Spike's Data Discovery analysis with supplemental cuts."*

- **Scale** — 608,121 individuals and 722,086 bookings since 2012, worth £169.1m in total booking value.
- **Retention is structurally weak** — 90% of customers have booked only once, and on average only ~3% of any year's bookers return the following year.
- **Revenue is concentrated** — The top 20% of customers drive 65% of total revenue (Pareto).
- **Direct now leads on channel** — Direct has overtaken Agent as the top booking source (51–57% since 2023); OTA has grown rapidly since 2021.
- **UK is the weakest market for repeat bookings** — 83.6% of UK customers book only once — the lowest repeat rate of any major source market, and the biggest single retention opportunity.
- **The base is skewed to the extremes** — First Timers (282k) and Lapsed (288k) dominate the segmentation; genuinely Loyal customers are rare (1,346).
- **International markets over-index on value and affluence** — Canada, Australia and the USA show higher ABV and CLTV than the UK; 60% of Australian customers sit in the top 3 IRSAD affluence deciles.
- **Marketability has declined sharply since 2021** — Email marketing permission on new bookers has fallen from ~98% to ~8%, limiting how much of the recent customer base can be remarketed to.

# 2. Key Markets

- **UK** — Largest volume base but weakest loyalty — 83.6% book only once, the lowest repeat rate of any major market. Shortest lead times; heavily concentrated around Edinburgh and Glasgow departures.
- **USA** — Second-largest by volume (63k customers); highest ABV among the "big four" (£226); skews toward higher-income and older age bands than the US national average. Concentrated around NY/NJ, DC and the West Coast.
- **Canada** — Smaller volume (14k customers) but the highest ABV (£310) and CLTV (£430) of any major market; Toronto-dominated; income-decile data skews affluent.
- **Australia** — High-value: £271 ABV, £375 CLTV; strikingly affluent — 60% of customers sit in the top 3 IRSAD deciles.
- **Smaller markets** — Germany, China/Hong Kong, France, Spain, Switzerland — lower volumes, mid-range ABV.
- **Channel mix is shifting structurally** — Direct has overtaken Agent as the leading source (51–57% since 2023); Agent has collapsed from ~54% (2015) to ~8–9%; OTA has grown from near-zero to ~35–40% since 2021.

# 3. Key Audiences

- **The base is barbelled** — First Timers (282k) and Lapsed (288k) dominate; genuinely Loyal customers (6+ bookings) are vanishingly rare (1,346).
- **Revenue is concentrated** — The top 20% of customers drive 65% of revenue (Pareto).
- **Affluence profiling (OAC)** — Heavily over-indexed on "Multicultural and Educated Urbanites" (penetration index +77 vs. baseline); "Retired Professionals" and "Semi/UnSkilled Workforce" under-index sharply (-10, -11).
- **UK geography** — London and Scotland massively over-index on penetration (indices of 18 and 37 vs. baseline); North East, Wales and Northern Ireland are barely penetrated (indices ~8–10).
- **Nationality shapes behaviour** — Americans skew heavily to Edinburgh departures (69.9%); other nationalities spread more evenly across hubs.

# 4. Key Opportunities

- **Grow the transaction, not just retention** — Upsell/cross-sell within a single trip has more headroom than converting one-time bookers into repeaters.
- **UK retention is the single biggest lever** — Largest market, weakest repeat rate — even a modest improvement moves more absolute customers than fixing already-stronger international rates.
- **Reactivate the Lapsed segment** — 288k people — larger than the entire active base — rather than only chasing new acquisition.
- **Lean into affluent international markets** — Canada and Australia warrant premium-oriented offers given materially higher ABV/CLTV.
- **Referrals and market affinities** — Flagged as under-exploited acquisition channels, especially given how concentrated the base already is in a few highly-penetrated pockets.
- **Fix the marketability gap** — Email permission on new bookers fell from ~98% to ~8% since 2021 — most of the recent, growing customer base is currently unreachable by email.

# 5. Deeper Cuts

*Slide framing text: "A few more specific patterns worth drawing out beyond the headline numbers."*

- **Product — there's a dominant second tour** — "Loch Ness, Glencoe & the Highlands" is the top second-tour pick regardless of which tour came first — 28.9% of Outlander Adventure customers, 31.4% of Loch Lomond/Kelpies/Stirling customers and 30.0% of St Andrews & Fishing Villages customers all choose it next. The one exception is Skye & Eilean Donan Castle customers, who skew toward The Complete Loch Ness Experience (21.9%) instead.
- **Departure hub — nationality drives hub choice more than expected** — Hong Kong (81.8%), Switzerland (79.2%) and Germany (75.4%) over-index on Edinburgh even more than the USA (69.9%). China is the outlier, with 21.4% departing from London — far higher than any other nationality. Canada is the most spread-out market (58.5% Edinburgh, 21.6% Glasgow, 11.8% Inverness).
- **Time to second booking — three distinct peaks, not one** — Month 1, month 11, and months 23–24 all spike. The month-1 spike is likely inflated by same-holiday bookings split in two (per the Supplemental cut); the 11- and 23-month peaks look like a genuine annual/biennial return cadence.

# 6. Open Questions for Spike

*Slide framing text: "Gaps in the current cuts worth asking Spike to close in the next round."*

- **Who exactly is in the top 20%?** — We can infer from segment/country patterns (Loyal, Canada/USA/Australia skew), but a direct crosstab of the top-20%-by-CLTV cohort against country, segment and OAC group would confirm it.
- **What's the same-holiday-adjusted repeat rate?** — The headline "90% book once" and "~3% YOY repeat" figures likely include some same-holiday, multi-booking noise. What do retention numbers look like once that's stripped out?
- **What would a market affinity analysis show?** — Flagged by Spike as a next step but not yet delivered — what could an affinity or look-alike model surface for acquisition targeting?
- **Why does China skew so heavily to London?** — 21.4% of Chinese customers depart from London, far above any other nationality (next highest is Australia at 15.8%). Product mix, partner/agent relationships, or a different customer profile?

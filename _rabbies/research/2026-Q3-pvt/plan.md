## What we are trying to learn

Five questions. Each changes a recommendation depending on the answer.

1. **Do private tour buyers want a fully custom tour, or a private version of an existing scheduled tour?** This is the question the initiative was raised to answer. A custom build and a private booking option on existing PDPs are different products, different effort, and different pages. Nothing should be scoped until this is answered.

2. **Is the private-tour pull a need state held by the existing four personas, or a distinct decision pattern?** `personas.md` records it as a need state layered on the four, primary home P1, secondary thread P2, and marks that exclusion for revisiting if this research runs. The decision rests on 2 private-tour buyers ever interviewed. If discovery, decision pattern and value exchange turn out to diverge from all four, the exclusion falls and the reach and impact scores on every private tour initiative are wrong.

3. **What triggers the switch from a scheduled tour to a private one?** Candidates already visible in the evidence are group size, occasion, accessibility need and privacy. The answer determines what qualification content belongs on the scheduled tour PDP, which is where the switch would have to be caught.

4. **Where does the enquiry to booking handoff lose people?** Private tours run through an enquiry path rather than a booking flow, with a consultation pilot on Microsoft Bookings. The answer decides whether the fix is site side or sales side, and those have different owners.

5. **Why do groups who could have booked a private tour dismiss Rabbie's before enquiring at all?** One documented direct revenue miss: a 13-guest group dismissed Rabbie's because Facebook groups suggested day tours only. If that pattern repeats, private tours are a discovery and positioning problem before they are a conversion problem, and the work belongs with Salience and brand rather than with CRO.

---

## Method

Moderated depth interviews with private tour bookers, recruited through the enquiry to booking handoff. Moderator guide already produced.

**The structural limitation, stated up front.** Recruiting through the handoff reaches people who converted. It cannot reach enquirers who dropped out, and it cannot reach groups who never enquired, which is exactly the population question 5 is about. This was identified as a gap before fielding and has not been solved. Anything this study produces about non-converting private tour demand is inference, not evidence, and must be labelled as such.

**TO CONFIRM.** Whether any second instrument is running alongside the interviews, for example enquiry record analysis or a question added to the consultation call.

## What would change our mind

The current working hypothesis is the one recorded in `personas.md`: private tours are a need state layered on the existing four personas rather than a fifth persona, and the buyers are mostly P1 with a secondary thread of P2, the large-family or special-event group.

That is overturned if participants show a discovery path and a decision pattern that map to none of the four. Divergence on the private-tour interest alone is not enough, because that is what a need state looks like. The test is whether they find Rabbie's differently, decide differently, and value different things.

A second hypothesis worth stating so it can fail. The initiative assumes private tours are a demand that Rabbie's is failing to capture. If participants describe private tours as a fallback taken only because a scheduled tour did not fit, the answer is scheduled tour availability and duration, not a private tour product. That would fold this work back into the mid-length duration gap.

---

## What this study cannot settle

Volume and revenue share. `business.md` records both as unknown, and interviews will not produce them. Sizing the opportunity needs Rabbie's enquiry and booking records, which is a data request, not a research question.

## Closed out, 18/09/2026

Fieldwork ran 2 to 16 September 2026, 6 participants, all converted. Findings in `findings.md`.
This section answers the plan against what came back. It does not restate the findings.

### The five questions

**1. Fully custom, or a private version of a scheduled tour? Answered, and the question was
slightly wrong.** Both shapes appear and the distinction did not turn out to be the decision. AM
deliberately used a published Rabbie's group tour as a skeleton and wanted only the arrangements
made. KA arrived with a self-built itinerary and wanted it corrected. CP wanted a trip created and
got what he experienced as the public tour relabelled. The product is the same route inventory in
every case. What separates a good private tour from a poor one is curation, not customisation. That
reframes the scoping decision: this is not a build choice between two products, it is a question
about who designs the trip and with what knowledge.

**2. Need state, or a distinct decision pattern? Answered, and neither option was right.** It is a
role. Recorded as R1 The Appointed Organiser in `personas.md` 17/09/2026, with the reasoning on the
page. 5 of 6 were organising for a party they did not fully control and mapped to two existing
personas at once rather than forming a fifth cluster. The plan's test was whether they find Rabbie's
differently, decide differently and value different things. They decide differently and value
different things. They do not find Rabbie's differently, which is why this is not a persona.

The consequence the plan anticipated does not follow. The exclusion holds, no fifth persona was
created, and the reach and impact scores on private tour initiatives are not automatically wrong.
They are unsizeable, which is a different problem. See question 4.

**3. What triggers the switch to private? Answered.** Group composition, not taste. The constraints
named were mobility, diet, age spread from 11 to 80s, motion sickness across a party, and a refusal
to drive on the left. Nobody switched for luxury. The qualification content that belongs on the
scheduled tour PDP is therefore about party constraints, not about a better product.

**4. Where does the enquiry to booking handoff lose people? Not answered, and it cannot be from this
study.** Every participant converted. This was flagged before fielding and the flag was correct.
A related and unexpected finding: the enquiry to booking rate for private tours is not reported
anywhere, so the handoff cannot currently be measured even with the right sample. That is a data
request, not a research question, and it is now the missing instrument for the whole programme.

**5. Why do groups dismiss Rabbie's before enquiring? Not answered.** Same structural reason. The
one adjacent signal is F9: 2 of 6 assumed Rabbie's was Scotland only, and one of them gave a week of
European business to another operator as a direct result. That corroborates the pattern behind the
13-guest miss without evidencing it.

### The two hypotheses

**Need state hypothesis: superseded rather than falsified.** See question 2.

**Private tours as a fallback from a scheduled tour that did not fit: falsified.** No participant
described private as a second choice. 5 of 6 went to private because the group made a scheduled tour
unworkable, which is a positive selection rather than a fallback. This does not fold into the
mid-length duration gap.

### What the study produced that the plan did not anticipate

Accommodation. 4 of 4 multi-day bookers had a problem, four different problems, with no dissent in
the subgroup, which is unusual in this evidence base. It is now the highest impact item in
`strategy/2026-09_private-tours-service-model.md` and nothing in this plan predicted it.

## The plan, up front

Rabbie's makes more money in 2027 by selling more seats on departures it already runs, starting with multi-day in Scotland. Most of the cost of a departure is fixed, so an extra passenger on a running tour is close to pure margin. That is management's own statement of the business. The plan has three levers: more seats sold, lower cost to sell, and lower cost to serve. It also has one guardrail: no volume growth where the margin is unknown.

It runs in four phases. **October** is for measuring and deciding. We cannot show profit today, because margin, load factor and commission rates are not held anywhere in the project. **October to December** ships the cheap multi-day and post-booking fixes before the January booking peak. **January to June 2027** builds the Buy Box, Direct Packages and the product data pipeline. **July to December 2027** scales what worked into more markets and more of the catalogue.

Every party has a list below. Rabbie's carries the most decisions, and nine of them have no owner today. Assigning those owners is the first action in the plan.

**The single piece of information that decides whether this plan can be judged on profit:** contribution per departure at current load factor, split by product. With it, every item below can be ranked on margin. Without it, "profitability" in this plan means seats and cost to sell, which are proxies.

**The contract measure is out of line with these goals.** The Sunny Lemons contract is measured on a 1% or better improvement in conversion rate. The SOW does not say whether that is relative or absolute. A site-wide rate is dominated by day tours and can rise while multi-day falls. The renewal proposal should replace it with multi-day conversion and seats sold (see Sunny Lemons actions).

## The three levers and the guardrail

| Lever | Why it moves profit | Main items |
|---|---|---|
| **More seats sold on running departures** | Fixed cost per departure, per management. Extra passengers are close to pure margin | Accommodation on multi-day tour pages (OP-03.2), cross-sell on confirmation (OP-06.1), late availability page (Init. 8), register interest on cancelled and new-date tours (Init. 15), listing order towards under-filled departures (OP-02.1), multi-tour basket (OP-06.3) |
| **Lower cost to sell** | Paid spend is £706k against a £605k target, with ROAS and cost per booking behind plan | Product-tagged conversions and a product and market split in paid (OP-10.5), multi-day spend timing test (A17), mobile tour page (Init. 12), owned reviews (Init. 10) |
| **Lower cost to serve** | Staff time spent on data entry and on reassuring customers is cost without revenue | Product data pipeline (OP-01.4), fewer accommodation scenarios (OP-03.1), "what happens next" and the day-before SMS (OP-08.1, 08.4) |
| **Guardrail: no growth where margin is unknown** | Private tours use dedicated vehicles, drivers and consultant hours. The 2027 forward book is up 116.5%, so resource will flow there anyway | No acquisition spend into private tours until the margin comparison exists (`assumptions.md` A20). Qualification at first contact is allowed (OP-11.1) |

**Timing that shapes everything.** Longer tours book early in the year. 7-day tours took 23% of their 2025 bookings in January, and March is the peak booking month overall. Anything meant to lift multi-day in 2027 has to be live by early January 2027. That is why Phase 1 is short and content-led.

**One open question could redirect the seat lever.** If popular Scotland departures already sell out in peak season, converting more traffic to those dates adds nothing. The work then becomes shifting demand to shoulder season and under-filled routes, which is merchandising rather than conversion. Load factor by route and month settles it. It is the first ask in Phase 0.

## The critical path

Five chains decide whether 2027 lands. A slip at the start of any of them moves its end.

1. **Profit visibility:** R2 load factor, contribution and commission → R3 Scotland target → R7 scorecard → every later priority call.
2. **Multi-day before January:** O1 cut scenarios → S3 spec → E4 accommodation content → L4 test → T7 Direct Packages rollout.
3. **Product data:** E1 time log and E2 locale answer → S2 attributes → T6 scope → R8 funding → T10 build → filters, availability, compare.
4. **Selling the second tour:** F1 finance decision → M4 cross-sell now → T13 basket later.
5. **Reachable customers:** M1 and V2 permission cause → M8 post-booking sequence with offers → M11 rebook segmentation.

---

## How the plan is run

- **Monthly strategic review**, as the SOW already provides, reports against the 2027 scorecard, not against activity.
- **Every item gets a measure and a baseline before it is scoped** (OP-12.1). The measures in this plan are proposed and need agreeing.
- **One owner per decision**, named by 10/10/2026.
- **Cheap and reversible first.** Build only when volume justifies it. Rabbie's has tended to over-engineer responses to small feedback volumes.
- **Any change to the plan is written down**, with the reason, in the monthly review notes.

# Growth initiatives from the all agency workshop

**Date:** 24/09/2026
**Author:** Joe Collingwood, Sunny Lemons
**Status:** Draft v1, for discussion with Michelle and Alex
**Sources:** All agency workshop post-it wall, September 2026 (votes as recorded); State of the nation deck (Michelle); Rabbie's Customer Insights full analysis; UX tracker synced 05/09/2026; booker and private tours research
**Boundary:** Vote counts are summed across groups that raised the same problem, so totals exceed any single note. Votes show what the room cared about, not what the customer data ranks. Where the two disagree, the data should win.

---

## The naming structure

`GI-0n · <the problem in customer terms>`, with sub-items `GI-0n.1`, `.2` and so on.

Each sub-item carries two tags:

- **Now** or **Next**, for sequencing
- **Opex** or **Capex**, for how it gets funded

This gives Alex a cheap-and-fast list, gives Michelle a batch view rather than 17 disconnected initiative numbers, and lets the larger builds be put forward as capital projects rather than agency spend.

## The framing point to make first

Michelle's note was to reduce spend on new customers and focus on retention. The customer data does not support next-year retention as the lever:

- About half of "repeat" bookings are the same holiday, booked within 14 days of the first tour ending
- 37 to 41% of second bookings happen on the same day as the first, and a further 13 to 15% within a week
- UK repeat is 16.4%, the lowest of all markets, against 24.0% Canada, 23.1% Australia, 21.0% US
- Email permission on new bookers is 8.05% in 2026

The retention opportunity is almost entirely **inside the current trip**. That reframing makes GI-01 the largest commercial item on the wall, and it costs nothing to say.

## GI-01 · Booking more than one tour
**34 votes**

**Problem.** No basket. One tour per transaction, blocked by finance and tech restrictions. No way to shortlist several holidays and book them together. No attraction bundling.

**Opportunity.** Customers already buy a second tour, we just make them do it twice. 37 to 41% of second bookings happen the same day as the first, and Loch Ness & Glencoe is the top second tour after three of our best sellers at 29 to 31%. Capturing the second booking in the same session raises order value with no extra acquisition spend, against inventory already secured.

| Ref | Action | Tags |
|---|---|---|
| GI-01.1 | Confirmation page and confirmation email cross-sell using the known first-to-second tour pairs. No basket required | Now, Opex |
| GI-01.2 | Multi-tour basket. Needs the finance restriction resolved first | Next, Capex |

**Existing initiatives:** 13 (related tours), 10c (invite to tour)
**Measure:** Share of bookings containing more than one tour; second bookings taken in the same session

## GI-02 · Knowing where you will sleep
**49 votes, the largest cluster**

**Problem.** Accommodation is invisible until inside the booking flow, the scenarios are too complex operationally, options are unclear, and Direct Packages is not everywhere.

**Opportunity.** Multi Day is the biggest drag on the business at -20% to budget and the weakest 2027 forward gain at +32.2%. Accommodation opacity is the top multi-day pain point in the research, and a 60% drop sits at that step in the flow. Moving accommodation type, price range and inclusions onto the tour page qualifies people before they commit. Simplifying the option set is an operational decision that needs no build at all.

| Ref | Action | Tags |
|---|---|---|
| GI-02.1 | Accommodation content on multi-day tour pages | Now, Opex |
| GI-02.2 | Operations decision to cut the number of accommodation scenarios | Now, Opex |
| GI-02.3 | Direct Packages rollout and the accommodation selector | Next, Capex |

**Existing initiatives:** 2 (Direct Packages 2.0), 2a (booking flow), 2c (accommodation on tour page)
**Measure:** Drop-off at the accommodation step; multi-day conversion

## GI-03 · Choosing between tours
**27 votes**

**Problem.** Decision fatigue on listing pages, tour card design, filters that do not surface a tour that fits, listing pages assuming destination knowledge, Scotland crowding out other regions, one tour page template for every tour type.

**Opportunity.** Most of the 92% drop-off sits here. This is also where Scotland scaling and non-Scotland growth collide: if Scotland dominates listing pages by default, non-Scotland products cannot grow whatever we spend on them.

| Ref | Action | Tags |
|---|---|---|
| GI-03.1 | Tour card content, merchandising rules for listing order, copy that does not assume prior knowledge | Now, Opex |
| GI-03.2 | Listing page data structure and filter rebuild | Next, Capex |
| GI-03.3 | Tour page templates by tour type | Next, Capex |

**Existing initiatives:** 1, 1a (navigation and IA), 5 (PLP data structure), 12 (mobile tour page)
**Measure:** Listing to tour page progression; filter use to tour view; zero-result searches

## GI-04 · Keeping what I found
**24 votes**

**Problem.** No shortlist, no comparison, the site forgets preferences and dates, nothing to save for later, no way to build "my trip".

**Opportunity.** Customers compare with spreadsheets, browser tabs and emailed lists of links today, and 52% of multi-day buyers view two or more tours before purchase. Comparison support is also the natural home for lead capture on a long lead-time purchase.

| Ref | Action | Tags |
|---|---|---|
| GI-04.1 | Recently viewed, and persisting passengers and dates within a session | Now, Opex |
| GI-04.2 | Shortlist, compare and shareable lists | Next, Capex |

**Existing initiatives:** 4 (persist passengers and dates), 13 (personalisation), 10a (split it)
**Measure:** Tours viewed per session; return-to-site rate on saved lists

## GI-05 · The gap between booking and travelling
**43 votes**

**Problem.** No warm-up or excitement building, no SMS, no customer portal, the old manage-booking page carries security issues, it is not clear what happens next after booking, no post-purchase flow for long lead-time bookers.

**Opportunity.** Lead times run from 28 days to over 150 by departure hub, and 85 days from Australia. That is months of silence in which we neither reassure nor sell. This is the cheapest cluster on the wall relative to its vote count, and it protects NPS as well as revenue.

| Ref | Action | Tags |
|---|---|---|
| GI-05.1 | Post-booking sequence segmented by lead time | Now, Opex |
| GI-05.2 | Day-before SMS with pickup point, time and driver-guide name | Now, Opex |
| GI-05.3 | "What happens next" content on confirmation | Now, Opex |
| GI-05.4 | Customer portal, which also retires the security risk on the current manage-booking page | Next, Capex |

**Existing initiatives:** 10a (auth and booking management, split it), 10e (post-tour email)
**Measure:** Inbound contacts asking whether a booking is confirmed; time from booking to accommodation confirmation

## GI-06 · Completing the booking
**31 votes**

**Problem.** Too many steps and lingering doubts, split payment not visible, no sale price in the buy box, no seats-left indicator, availability not shown on the tour page, currency limited to GBP and USD despite Canadian and Australian markets.

**Opportunity.** Canada has the highest ABV at £310 and Australia £271, and we ask both to pay in a foreign currency. Currency and payment visibility are small changes aimed at our two highest-value markets, which fits the objective of understanding target markets and the opportunity within them.

| Ref | Action | Tags |
|---|---|---|
| GI-06.1 | Currency display for CAD and AUD | Now, Opex |
| GI-06.2 | Split payment visibility and seats-left indicator | Now, Opex |
| GI-06.3 | Booking flow rebuild and deposits | Next, Capex |

**Existing initiatives:** 2a, 3, 3a, 3b (Buy Box), 9 (deposits)
**Measure:** Step completion through the flow; checkout to purchase on multi-day

## GI-07 · Capturing intent we currently lose
**24 votes**

**Problem.** No lead generation, no abandoned basket capture or retargeting, no referral mechanism, and a rebook offer that is one generic code for everybody.

**Opportunity.** Most customers arrive through recommendation and we have no infrastructure behind it. A large share of long lead-time visitors are not ready to book and we let them leave anonymously. This is the cluster that most directly answers the instruction to reduce spend on acquiring new customers.

| Ref | Action | Tags |
|---|---|---|
| GI-07.1 | Abandoned basket flow and retargeting | Now, Opex |
| GI-07.2 | Referral prompt at confirmation | Now, Opex |
| GI-07.3 | Segment the rebook offer instead of one generic code | Now, Opex |
| GI-07.4 | Save for later and lead capture in the funnel | Next, Capex |

**Blocker to name:** email permission on new bookers is 8.05%. Establish the cause, a tracking change or a real consent collapse, before anything is built on top of that base.
**Existing initiatives:** 10d (referral), 10e (post-tour email)
**Measure:** Recovered baskets; referred sessions, then referred bookings

## GI-08 · Showing what makes us different
**27 votes**

**Problem.** Driver-guides are not visible on the site, USPs are not explained to new visitors, product differentiation is weak, destination detail is thin, and generic landscape content can read as a walking tour.

**Opportunity.** The guide accounts for roughly 90% of experience quality by customers' own account and is one of only two genuine differentiators, yet it is almost absent from the website. With 42.6% of direct bookings from the US and a first-visit-heavy profile, the proposition has to work without prior brand knowledge. This is content, not engineering, so it is the fastest quality lever available.

| Ref | Action | Tags |
|---|---|---|
| GI-08.1 | Guide profiles and stories on tour pages | Now, Opex |
| GI-08.2 | USP block for first-time visitors | Now, Opex |
| GI-08.3 | Destination and attraction detail on the tours that need it | Now, Opex |

**Blocker to name:** guide content comes from operations, who were not in the room. It needs a named person to carry it back.
**Existing initiatives:** 2b (guide module)
**Measure:** Tour page to availability check, with the guide module against without

## GI-09 · Speaking to each market properly
**26 votes**

**Problem.** No market or user-need specific landing pages, US SEO, cross-geo signals confusing Google, no Canada or Australia focus, feedback signals dominated by Scotland day tours.

**Opportunity.** The US delivers 42.6% of direct bookings at 1.86% conversion against the UK's 16.2% at 0.78%. Quality, not volume, is the lever, and we currently use one voice for all markets. Landing page templates also make every future campaign cheaper to launch, which matters more in a cost-cutting year than any single page.

| Ref | Action | Tags |
|---|---|---|
| GI-09.1 | Market-specific copy on the highest-traffic pages | Now, Opex |
| GI-09.2 | Cross-geo signal fixes | Now, Opex |
| GI-09.3 | Landing page template system | Next, Capex |

**Existing initiatives:** 8, 8a (landing pages), 14 (local content), 6 (European booker research)
**Measure:** Conversion by market; landing page to tour page progression by campaign type

## GI-10 · Freeing ecommerce capacity
**26 votes**

**Problem.** Roughly 65% of ecommerce team time goes on updating products by hand despite the data living in Traverse, the tour page and CMS build is manual and complex, CMS backend functionality limits the team, and there is no aggregated data to serve propensity.

**Opportunity.** This is the cost-cutting argument in reverse. Around two thirds of a team's month is spent on product data and the September audit says the data is still not correct. Fixing the pipeline releases roughly 1.9 FTE of capacity into merchandising, which is exactly what driving volume from already-secured inventory requires. No headcount added.

| Ref | Action | Tags |
|---|---|---|
| GI-10.1 | Time log separating first-pass work from rework | Now, Opex |
| GI-10.2 | Locale duplication check across en-gb, en-us and en-eu | Now, Opex |
| GI-10.3 | Traverse to CMS integration | Next, Capex |

**Existing initiatives:** none. This is a gap in the current list.
**Measure:** Days per month on product data; audit defect count

## GI-11 · Private tour intent on the scheduled catalogue
**1 vote, but commercially weighted**

**Problem.** People who want a private tour also go through scheduled tours, and nobody has investigated why.

**Opportunity.** Private tours are -28% to budget but +24% year on year, with the 2027 forward book up 116.5%. The research says the buyer is an appointed organiser buying delegated responsibility for a group, and that the same role appears on parties booking scheduled seats. A qualification question at first contact costs almost nothing and routes group enquiries to the right product.

| Ref | Action | Tags |
|---|---|---|
| GI-11.1 | Detection at enquiry: party size, age spread, a named constraint | Now, Opex |

**Gate:** no acquisition spend into the private pipeline until we have a margin comparison against filled scheduled seats and an enquiry-to-booking rate. Neither exists today. Spend into the current pipeline would turn a quality problem into a reputation problem.
**Existing initiatives:** 7 (private tours research, delivered 18/09/2026)
**Measure:** Enquiry to booking rate for private tours

## Two things to fix in how the list is run

1. **Outcomes.** Only one note asked what impact we expect, and it took two votes. Every GI needs one measure and a baseline before it is scoped. The measures above are proposed by Sunny Lemons, not agreed.
2. **Sequencing between agencies.** The one unresolved conflict on the wall, that CRO work breaks SEO and vice versa, is a sequencing problem between agencies rather than an initiative. It needs an owner and a rule, not a ticket.

## Open questions

- Does the finance restriction on multiple tours per transaction have a workaround short of a full basket build? GI-01.2 is the largest single opportunity and it is blocked on this.
- What is the cause of the 8.05% email permission rate? It gates GI-07 entirely.
- Who carries guide content back to operations? GI-08 stalls without a named person.
- Which of these can be capitalised under Rabbie's own accounting treatment? The Now/Next and Opex/Capex tags here are a proposal, not a determination.

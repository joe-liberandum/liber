## The plan, up front

Rabbie's has asked for the biggest impact, cost-effectively and with minimal complexity, for the rest of 2026 and into 2027. The plan answers with four moves, in this order.

1. **Sell the second tour before they travel.** Booked customers can be emailed with offers, so nothing now stands in the way. Cross-sell on the confirmation page and email (QW-15) is the most cost-effective action in the plan. The pre-arrival email sequence (SP-08) follows by December, before the January multi-day peak. Salience adds the paid channel: a post-booking campaign with second-tour offers, run against a holdout (SP-27), and excluding recent bookers from acquisition spend (QW-24). All of these sell seats on departures already running, which is the 2026 focus exactly. Paid targeting of booked customers needs its own legal basis confirmed (DC-18). Email consent does not automatically cover it.
2. **Fix the tour page, where the biggest leak is.** 82% of people who reach a tour page do not reach checkout. The tour page actions move up: the mobile layout (QW-12), accommodation content on multi-day tours (SP-04), cancellation terms, inclusions and the full price shown early (SP-25), end dates (QW-11), the USP block (QW-13) and owned reviews (SP-19). All but owned reviews land in 2026.
3. **Take the doubt out of the commitment point.** 53% are lost between guest details and payment. The working explanation is that doubts build up through the journey and land at the moment of commitment. That points upstream to the tour page, and to a summary at guest details. First comes a cheap check that the payment step is tracked end to end (QW-23). Then a test of the hypothesis by January (SP-15). Then the booking flow work inside Direct Packages (LI-02).
4. **Start the product data foundation now and finish it by March 2027.** Tech capacity reduces in 2027 and the date is unknown. That makes starting early more important, not less. It is also the dependency for the listing page rebuild (LI-12) and for availability and seats left in the Buy Box (LI-04). It is a Tech build, but it needs the attribute definition from UX (SP-02) and Ecommerce's answer on how products are maintained (QW-04), both by 31/10/2026.

**The listing page is the third leak, not the first.** It loses 52%, against 82% on the tour page. The cheap listing page fixes (SP-06) stay in 2026, and the rebuild (LI-12) stays in Q2 2027, carried by the foundation. But the evidence now says the tour page matters more.

Alongside these, the paid split by product and market (SP-10) and merchandising under-filled departures (SP-24) are among the most cost-effective small projects, and both land in 2026.

**Five more actions reach people the plan was missing.** All land by March 2027, and four of them in 2026.

- **Customers already on a tour:** driver-guides and vans offer the next tour (QW-25).
- **People who leave from a tour page:** retargeting by the tour they viewed (SP-28), and "email me this tour" for multi-day (SP-29).
- **People who reach guest details and stop:** abandoned basket emails to people who have already given an email address (SP-30).
- **People with a question at the moment of decision:** phone and chat on multi-day tour pages and at guest details (QW-26).

The guides action and the abandoned basket action rank among the most cost-effective in the plan.

**Tech-heavy work in the second half of 2027 is a 2028 candidate** unless Rabbie's confirms the capacity. That covers the multi-tour basket, the account and compare build, and the landing page templates. Cross-sell on confirmation and before arrival captures most of the basket's 2027 value without a build.

**Two pieces of information would most change this plan.** Tech capacity through 2027 (DC-17) sets the sequence, and it is not yet known. The funnel split by day tour and multi-day (QW-09) sets what to fix first. The three figures are blended, and the two products lose people in different places.

**Five readings.**

1. **The tour page is the biggest leak, and it is real loss.** The 82% is measured per person, and these people leave the site. They are not moving on to another tour. So this is the largest point at which Rabbie's loses people it has already paid to acquire. None of them has reached a basket, so abandoned basket flows cannot reach them. Only retargeting and the tour page itself can. Where they go next is not known. That is the non-booker research gap, which is a separate commission.
2. **The commitment point is a hypothesis, stated so it can fail.** The thinking is that doubts build up through the journey and land at payment, rather than the guest details page causing them. That fits the standing judgement that qualification belongs on the tour page, because late surprises cause abandonment. Two other explanations have not been ruled out. One is price shock when the full total appears. The other is measurement: if payment happens on a provider's page and the return is not tracked, part of the 53% is not loss at all. QW-23 checks the second in days. SP-15 tests the hypothesis. Recorded as `assumptions.md` A21.
3. **Funnel steps multiply.** A 10% relative gain at any step adds 10% more bookings. So the size of a leak says where the headroom is, not which fix to do first. The priority score already chooses fixes by impact per unit of cost.
4. **The three figures are blended across day tours and multi-day.** They are different products that lose people at different points. On the Edinburgh export, 13 in 100 multi-day checkouts reach payment, against 38 in 100 for day tours. The multi-day loss at the commitment point is probably worse than 53%. QW-09 splits the figures before any fix is sized.
5. **Navigation worked.** More people now reach listing pages. The before-and-after figure is still worth pulling, because it is the evidence that initiative 1 delivered.

## How work is scored

Two numbers per action. **Impact** answers "biggest impact". **Priority** answers "cost-effectively and with minimal complexity".

| Factor | Scale | What earns the top score |
|---|---|---|
| **Seats and revenue** | 0 to 3 | Sells more seats on departures already running, at a measured loss point or to a customer already won |
| **Margin** | 0 to 3 | Removes a measured cost at scale, or sells without acquisition cost |
| **Focus fit** | 0 to 2 | 2 for secured inventory (2026), multi-day or Scotland (2027). 1 for target markets or non-Scotland. 0 for neither |
| **Evidence** | Multiplier | Strong or audited 1.0. Directional or stated 0.75. Workshop only, single voice or untested 0.5 |
| **Lands** | Multiplier | In 2026, so it earns the rest of this year and the January peak, 1.0. Q1 2027, 0.8. Q2 2027, 0.5. Second half of 2027, 0.25 |
| **Effort** | Points | XS 1, S 2, M 3, L 5, XL 8 |
| **Complexity** | Points | 0 for one team with no dependency. 1 for two teams or one dependency. 2 for three or more teams, or several dependencies |

**Impact = (seats and revenue + margin + focus fit) × evidence × lands.** The highest possible is 8.

**Priority = impact ÷ (effort + complexity).** The higher the number, the more impact per unit of cost and complexity.

These are judgements of potential, not forecasts. Enablers are not scored. They are listed with what they unlock. Decisions are gates. The manage-booking security fix (QW-01) is mandatory.

## Sequencing rules

Priority orders the work within a team's capacity. Five rules override it, in this order.

1. **Mandatory first.** QW-01, the manage-booking security fix.
2. **Gates before what they gate.** DC-06 (accommodation scenarios) gates SP-04 and LI-02. DC-02 (load factor) gates SP-24. QW-23 (payment tracking) gates the commitment-point work. DC-18 (paid targeting basis) gates SP-27. DC-19 (abandoned basket basis) and QW-06 (the audit) gate SP-30. QW-06 also gates SP-28. The email consent gate for booked customers is resolved.
3. **Tech work lands before Tech capacity reduces.** Any action that needs Rabbie's developers is sequenced to finish by 31/03/2027 where it can, and by 30/06/2027 at the latest. This is why the product data foundation (LI-01) starts in October, despite a priority of 0.56. It is also why the listing page rebuild (LI-12) is designed in Q4, so that its build can start as soon as the data lands.
4. **Dependencies set the floor.** LI-12 cannot start its build until LI-01 delivers the data. LI-01 cannot be built well until SP-02 defines what the data must hold.
5. **2026 volume before year end.** Where two actions tie, the one that sells secured inventory before 31/12/2026 goes first.

# How Might We statements by journey stage

**Draft, v1, 22/09/2026.** For the all-agency session, 23 September 2026, Edinburgh. Not delivered.
Customer questions for pre-awareness, awareness, interest and desire, and book are as supplied.
Questions for pre-tour, tour and advocacy are drafted here from the research, and are marked as such.

---

## How to read this

Each stage carries one framing How Might We, which is the one that goes on the slide, and three to
four sharper ones underneath it for the activity itself.

Two rules to hold while running it. Every HMW has to be answered against a track, because day tours
and multi-day tours fail differently at every stage. Either run each one twice or assign it to a
track and say which on the slide. And every answer that comes off the wall should name the belief it
rests on and what would show that belief to be wrong, otherwise the output is a list of features with
no way to choose between them.

Note that the supplied set merges interest and desire. They are separated below, because interest
asks whether a trip that fits exists and desire asks whether this specific tour can be trusted, and
the two fail for different reasons. Merge them back on the slide if the day is short, but run the
activity twice.

---

## 1. Pre-awareness

> "Where should we go, and what kind of trip is this?"

**How might we be part of the conversation while someone is still deciding what kind of trip to take,
months before they are looking for an operator?**

- How might we help someone recognise a small group guided tour as the answer to a trip they have not
  yet shaped?
- How might we reach people where trip shape gets decided, when three of four personas make that
  decision off site?
- How might we know the early window is real before we spend against it?

**Track.** Multi-day only. Multi-day tours are the trip anchor and get decided here. Day tours do not
exist yet, because the trip has to exist before anything can fill it. Running this stage in both
tracks will waste half the room.

**Evidence behind it.** Booker interviews report consideration windows of months, and GA4 records
every one of them as a first-session booking. Australia shows an earlier booking pattern and
functions as the natural experiment. Confidence in the mechanism is high, confidence in the size of
the recoverable window is not.

---

## 2. Awareness

> "How do people like me actually do this trip?"

**How might we let someone see people like themselves on this trip, in the places they already trust,
before they reach our site?**

- How might we show a trip working for a specific party, when the constraints people carry are a
  walker user, a coeliac cousin, an 85 year old, or an age range from 11 to 70?
- How might we earn a place in the sources people actually use, given discovery is almost entirely
  Reddit, TripAdvisor, Rick Steves, personal recommendation and the OTAs?
- How might we make our credibility signals mean something to someone who does not know what B Corp
  or ABTOT is?
- How might we speak to a UK audience that already knows Scotland?

**Track.** Both, and the UK question is disproportionately a day tour question.

---

## 3. Interest

> "Do they run the kind of trip I want?"

**How might we let someone find out quickly whether a trip that fits them exists, without needing to
know how Rabbie's organises its catalogue?**

- How might we let a flexible planner in, when the front door currently demands a destination and a
  date before they have decided anything?
- How might we make sure a search never ends in nothing, when a dead end ends the session rather than
  producing a workaround?
- How might we let someone hold several tours in mind at once without building a spreadsheet?

**Track.** Both. 74% of homepage visitors use neither nav nor search.

---

## 4. Desire

> "Why Rabbie's?"

**How might we give someone enough proof on the page to believe this specific tour is worth their
days and will work for their party?**

- How might we make the guide visible before booking, when the guide accounts for roughly 90% of the
  experience afterwards?
- How might we answer qualification questions on the tour page, so nobody discovers the answer inside
  the booking flow?
- How might we make reviews build trust rather than break it?
- How might we be honest that only two things genuinely separate us, driver-guides and depth of
  service on logistics, and lead with those?

**Track.** Both, and this is where the divergence is widest. Interest and desire together carry
roughly 92% of drop-off.

---

## 5. Book

> "Exactly what am I committing to, and what does it cost?"

**How might we make the full commitment legible, in money, time and what is included, before someone
has to make it?**

- How might we ask people to decide less at checkout, by moving qualification upstream?
- How might we let someone commit progressively rather than all at once?
- How might we make a price someone can still account for months later?
- How might we let a party commit together when one person is paying for everybody?

**Track.** Mostly multi-day. The 60% drop at accommodation and the 60% drop at personal details are
both in the multi-day flow, and multi-day currently requires full payment upfront.

---

## 6. Pre-tour

> **Question drafted, not quoted.** "Is this actually happening, and what do I need to do?"

**How might we make someone feel the trip is being actively handled, across the months between
paying and travelling?**

- How might we confirm the parts still open fast enough that silence never has to be interpreted?
- How might we make sure what someone told us about their party reaches the person making the
  arrangements?
- How might we give someone one place to find their booking, when email is the system of record and
  fails at the top end of complexity?
- How might we help an organiser brief their own party, when they carry the blame personally?

**Track.** Multi-day, and acutely so on private tours. Day tours are booked close to the day and have
almost no pre-tour phase, which is itself worth saying in the room.

**Evidence behind it.** Months of silence between booking and accommodation confirmation, with one
customer going back through her email to check the booking was real. Silence reads as nothing
happening, and this persona will delegate logistics only if the process visibly feels managed. One
private booker finished with 80 to 90 linked emails and had to search them to recover her own
reference number. Another had replies land in junk repeatedly, including inside a thread she had
already replied to. A stated mobility need sat in an email chain and was not acted on when
accommodation was booked, producing a service failure on tour.

**Candidate quote for the slide** rather than a drafted question: the customer who went back through
her email to check the booking was real, or AM on the 80 to 90 email chain. Both are in
`research/2026-Q3-pvt/findings.md` and can be attributed by initials and segment.

**Owner.** Rabbie's operations and CRM, with Liberandum on lifecycle. Nothing on the UX backlog sits
in this stage.

## 7. Tour

> **Question drafted, not quoted.** "Was this worth the day I gave it?"

**How might we make sure the day delivers what the page promised, and that the guide is set up to do
the thing customers value most?**

- How might we make sure the itinerary someone bought is the itinerary they get?
- How might we give the guide what they need to read a group and adjust, when that is exactly what
  gets praised afterwards?
- How might we capture what happened on the day, so the guide gets the credit and problems reach the
  company?
- How might we end a tour as well as we start it, when drop-off in a different city is unclear?

**Track.** Both.

**Evidence behind it.** Six of six private bookers volunteered the driver-guide as the high point,
unprompted, and the praise was specific: reading the group and adjusting the day, physically
supporting two elderly travellers, rebooking an entire day when someone fell ill. Against that, a
tour sold as "fishing villages" plural visited one fishing village and one inland village, and a stop
presented as included was optional on the day. One van had no microphone, so the content was hard to
hear from the back row. One driver was explicit that leading walks was not his job.

**Caveat for the slide.** The itinerary over-promise is a single voice, from the only completed-tour
interview in the entire evidence base. Log it, do not build against it alone.

**Owner.** Rabbie's operations. Out of scope for this engagement, and worth ten minutes because it is
the input to both the guide module in desire and everything in advocacy.

## 8. Advocacy

> **Question drafted, not quoted.** "Who else should do this, and what do I do next?"

**How might we catch someone at the moment they most want to tell other people, when today that
moment passes with nothing asked of them?**

- How might we let someone praise their guide by name, when one customer wanted to and was never
  asked?
- How might we show a satisfied day tour customer that the multi-day catalogue exists?
- How might we support the advocacy people already give freely, without damaging it by formalising
  it?
- How might we reach anyone at all after the tour, when email permission on new bookers is 8.05%?

**Track.** Both, and the day to multi-day cross-sell is the specific opportunity.

**Evidence behind it.** One Segment A participant reported seeing people ask about Rabbie's in a
Scotland travel group at least a dozen times, and coaches new customers through booking unpaid and
untracked. There is no referral mechanism and no shareable wishlist. Post-tour surveys fire
inconsistently: two confirmed receiving one, two confirmed receiving none, two unknown. One of those
who received nothing had a service failure worth capturing, another actively wanted a survey in order
to praise her guide. A satisfied day tour customer, primed to rebook, did not know the multi-day
catalogue existed.

**Two caveats that belong on the slide.** The persona underneath this stage is the weakest in the set
at 65% confidence, and the Spike data sizes it small: 89.3% of customers have booked once, roughly 3%
of any year's bookers return the following year, and Spike counts 1,346 loyal customers at six or
more bookings against 281,902 first timers. Everything proposed here should stay cheap. Second,
whether formalising advocacy would capture it or damage it is unknown, and four initiatives currently
assume the former.

**The blocker rather than the idea.** Email permission at 8.05% gates the whole stage, and the cause
is still unknown, either a tracking convention change or a real consent collapse. It is cheap to
establish and nobody has asked.

## What the set does not cover

The four supplied questions come from participant quotes. The three drafted here do not, because no
quote was supplied for them. Pre-tour and advocacy both have real quotes available in the research
that would do the job better than a drafted question, and they are named above. Tour has only one
completed-tour interview behind it, so any quote used there carries a single-voice caveat.

# Part 1 · The original plan, tested against the workshop

## The answer, up front

The 2026 plan still stands. The workshop confirms and extends it, and the funnel figures since the workshop confirm it again.

- **More than half the room's votes, 173 of 314 (55%), land on problems an existing initiative already tackles.** The most-voted initiative is Direct Packages 2.0 and its sub-tasks, at 66 votes.
- **No workshop problem argues against any initiative on the tracker.**
- **Initiative 1 is delivered, and it worked.** Navigation now sends more people to listing pages.
- **The funnel figures back the original evidence.** Direct Packages 2.0 was prioritised on a 60% loss at personal details in the multi-day flow. The current figure is 53% lost between guest details and payment, blended across products. The tour page, where 82% of people leave the site, is where the original plan put the accommodation, guide, Buy Box and mobile tour page work.
- **The other 141 votes sit where the backlog was never aimed:** booking to travel, booking more than one tour, product data and operations, and markets. Those are gaps in coverage, not errors in direction.
- **Four things have changed shape since the workshop.**
  - Initiative 5 becomes the product data foundation. It starts in October 2026 and completes by March 2027, before Tech capacity reduces.
  - Related tours in initiative 13 becomes the pre-arrival upsell, now the first lever in the plan. It runs across the confirmation page, email and paid media.
  - The account, compare and multi-tour basket builds become 2028 candidates unless Rabbie's confirms Tech capacity.
  - Initiative 14 keeps its work but loses its UK-first rationale.
- **Thirteen items move earlier and six move later.** The earlier moves are led by the tour page and post-booking work. The later ones are the Tech-heavy builds that would otherwise land in the second half of 2027.

The framing has moved as well as the list. The 2026 plan aimed at site-wide conversion. Rabbie's now asks for the biggest impact, cost-effectively and with minimal complexity: volume from secured inventory in 2026, then Scotland and multi-day in 2027. Every initiative below is re-read against that. Scores, dates and owners are in `2026-09-24_2027-delivery-plan_v5.md`. This document explains where each original initiative went and why.

**A limit on the "confirms" claim.** The backlog was prepared as workshop input (`2026-09-21_workshop-initiatives-by-journey-phase_v1.md`). Where the room saw it, the overlap is corroboration from people who knew the plan, not an independent test. The customer research and the funnel figures are the stronger evidence.

## The framing point to make first

Michelle's note asked us to reduce spend on new customers and focus on retention. The customer data does not support next-year retention as the main lever:

- About half of "repeat" bookings are the same holiday, booked within 14 days of the first tour ending.
- 37 to 41% of second bookings happen on the same day as the first.
- UK repeat is 16.4%, the lowest of all markets, against 24.0% Canada, 23.1% Australia and 21.0% US.
- Email permission on new bookers is 8.05% in 2026, so most recent customers cannot be emailed.

The retention opportunity sits almost entirely **inside the current trip**: the second tour, the gap before travel, the day before. That is OP-06 and OP-08, and it costs no acquisition spend. Booked customers can be emailed with offers, confirmed 24/09/2026, so the pre-arrival upsell has no consent gate. Retention in the strict sense, bringing people back next year, still waits on the general permission rate being explained (OP-09.1).

## OP-01 · Product data held once, read everywhere
**18 direct votes. 110 votes across 24 post-its depend on it in whole or part.**

**From the wall.** CMS backend limits the ecommerce team (3). Tour page and CMS build too manual and complex (5). 65% of ecommerce team time spent updating products, although the data already sits in Traverse (8). Ecommerce bandwidth (2).

**Already established, not on the wall.** The product data structure has to be rebuilt. There are two reasons. First, so filters work and listing pages can serve every product properly. Second, for efficiency: the ecommerce team spend 50 to 60% of their time each month updating products by hand, re-keying data that already exists in Traverse into the CMS. That leaves room for human error and wrong product attribution.

**Evidence.** The September 2026 audit found 79 of 120 trade sheet products, 66%, with no listing page link a search engine can follow, 64 of 120 missing their taxonomy breadcrumb, and at least 48 links across 23 content entries sending visitors to the wrong market. Every product runs in three locales (en-gb, en-us, en-eu). Whether each is maintained once or three times is not known.

**The time figure needs one number, not two.** The wall says 65% and the working estimate is 50 to 60%. Both are estimates, and neither comes from a time log. The range is 50 to 65% until measured. Measuring it is OP-01.1, and it is the figure that makes the rebuild fundable.

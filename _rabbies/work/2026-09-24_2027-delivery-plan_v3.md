## The plan, up front

Rabbie's makes more money in 2027 by selling more seats on departures it already runs, starting with multi-day in Scotland. Most of the cost of a departure is fixed, so an extra passenger on a running tour is close to pure margin. That is management's own statement of the business. The plan has three levers: more seats sold, lower cost to sell, and lower cost to serve. It also has one guardrail: no volume growth where the margin is unknown.

The work is 72 actions in four buckets:

- **22 quick wins.** Small, no dependencies, able to start now. Most land before the January multi-day booking peak.
- **24 small projects.** Bounded pieces of work with one deliverable, usually waiting on one named thing.
- **11 larger initiatives.** Multi-team, multi-phase builds. Eight carry forward existing tracker initiatives from the 2026 plan. Two are new parents: the product data foundation, which absorbs initiative 5, and the multi-tour basket. One is Salience's market search programme.
- **15 decisions.** No build. Ten sit with Rabbie's leadership, and none has an owner today. Naming those owners is the first action in the plan (DC-01).

Profit cannot be shown yet. Margin, load factor and commission rates are not held anywhere in the project. Until DC-02 is answered, the plan is judged on seats sold and cost to sell, which are proxies.

**Where the 2027 impact is.** Ten actions carry most of it: cross-sell on confirmation (QW-15), Direct Packages 2.0 (LI-02), accommodation content on multi-day tour pages (SP-04), the paid split by product and market (SP-10), merchandising under-filled departures (SP-24), navigation (LI-03), the mobile tour page (QW-12), crawlable listing links (SP-16), owned reviews (SP-19) and the post-booking sequence (SP-08). Four of the top five wait on a Phase 0 gate: the accommodation scenario decision (two of them), load factor, and product-tagged conversions. The two new larger initiatives score low for 2027 because they land late, and their payoff is mostly in 2028. See "Impact on the 2027 focus".

## The three levers and the guardrail

| Lever | Why it moves profit | Main actions |
|---|---|---|
| **More seats sold on running departures** | Fixed cost per departure, per management. Extra passengers are close to pure margin | SP-04 accommodation content, QW-15 cross-sell, SP-18 late availability, QW-20 register interest, SP-24 merchandising under-filled departures, LI-06 multi-tour basket |
| **Lower cost to sell** | Paid spend is £706k against a £605k target, with ROAS and cost per booking behind plan | QW-07 re-baseline, SP-10 paid split, SP-11 early-window test, QW-12 mobile tour page, SP-19 owned reviews |
| **Lower cost to serve** | Staff time spent on data entry and on reassuring customers is cost without revenue | LI-01 product data foundation, DC-06 fewer accommodation scenarios, QW-14 what happens next, SP-09 day-before SMS |
| **Guardrail: no growth where margin is unknown** | Private tours use dedicated vehicles, drivers and consultant hours, and the 2027 forward book is up 116.5% | DC-13 holds acquisition spend until the margin comparison exists. QW-18 qualification at first contact is allowed |

**Timing.** 7-day tours took 23% of their 2025 bookings in January, and March is the peak booking month overall. Anything meant to lift multi-day in 2027 has to be live by early January 2027.

**One open question could redirect the seat lever.** If popular Scotland departures already sell out at peak, more conversion on those dates adds nothing, and the work shifts to shoulder season and under-filled routes. Load factor by route and month settles it (DC-02).

## Impact on the 2027 focus

Each quick win, small project and larger initiative is scored on the impact it could have on the 2027 focus: scale Scotland, grow multi-day, understand target markets, support non-Scotland. The objective behind the focus is more seats sold and more margin. Decisions are not scored. They are gates, and their value is the value of what they unlock.

### How it is scored

| Factor | Scale | What earns the top score |
|---|---|---|
| **Seats and revenue** | 0 to 3 | Sells more seats on multi-day or Scotland departures, at a measured loss point |
| **Margin** | 0 to 3 | Removes a measured cost at scale: paid spend over target, ecommerce hours, staff contacts. Or protects margin |
| **Focus fit** | 0 to 2 | 2 for multi-day or Scotland. 1 for target markets or non-Scotland. 0 for neither |
| **Evidence** | Multiplier | Strong or audited 1.0. Directional or stated 0.75. Workshop only, single voice or untested 0.5 |
| **Lands** | Multiplier | Full: live by 31/03/2027, so it catches the 2027 booking peak, 1.0. Part: by 30/06/2027, 0.6. Late: second half of 2027, 0.3 |

**2027 impact = (seats and revenue + margin + focus fit) × evidence × lands.** The highest possible is 8.

This is a judgement of potential, not a forecast. No action has a forecast value, because margin and load factor are not held. The evidence multiplier keeps weakly evidenced ideas from outranking proven ones on enthusiasm alone. The timing multiplier is what makes this a 2027 assessment: a strong idea that lands in November 2027 does little for 2027.

Each action falls into one of three classes. **Direct** actions are scored. **Enablers** produce no impact themselves but gate actions that do, so they are listed with what they unlock rather than ranked. The one **mandatory** action, the manage-booking security fix (QW-01), goes first whatever it scores.

### What the reassessment changes

1. **Owned reviews move earlier, to 31/03/2027.** It carries the strongest evidence on the tracker, at confidence 0.95. Landing in June scored 2.40. Landing by March scores 4.00.
2. **Three small projects are deferred to Phase 3.**
   - Persist passengers and dates (SP-17) folds into the shortlist and account work (LI-07). 0.90 as planned, 0.45 deferred.
   - Rebook segmentation (SP-22) waits until the email permission cause is known and repeat intent is better understood. Only about 3% of customers return the following year. 0.60 as planned, 0.30 deferred.
   - CAD and AUD currency (SP-23) goes ahead only if Finance shows currency is a real barrier. Canada and Australia are 8.9% and 3.9% of direct bookings. 0.60 as planned, 0.30 deferred.
3. **Three actions become validate-first.**
   - The Buy Box (LI-04) scores 1.20. That is because no usability study exists, not because the idea is weak. A Buy Box study joins the Q1 2027 research sprint before the availability and seats-left build.
   - Before building the day-before SMS (SP-09), Operations confirms how many day-before contacts it actually gets.
   - Deposits (LI-05) is in flight and prioritised by the board, so it continues. Its evidence is a single voice, so the CRO scorecard (SP-12) measures its effect on multi-day checkout from day one.
4. **The multi-tour basket is scoped by 31/03/2027**, not 30/06/2027. It carries the strongest seat evidence in the plan and scores 2.10 only because it lands late. Cross-sell on confirmation (QW-15, 7.00) captures most of the 2027 value now. An early scope lets Rabbie's decide whether a narrower version can land for summer.
5. **The product data foundation is sold on cost, not 2027 revenue.** It scores 2.10 because customers see it late, and its larger payoff is 2028. The 2027 case rests on ecommerce hours (QW-03) and data errors. Do not present it as a 2027 revenue lever.
6. **The two lowest-scoring quick wins stay, at the bottom of the queue.** Email links to the itinerary (0.50) and the guided-tour ad creative (1.00) each cost under two days. The ad creative folds into Salience's next creative refresh.
7. **Salience's workstream carries two of the four highest-impact small projects:** the paid split by product and market (SP-10, 5.25) and crawlable listing links (SP-16, 4.00). Owning both paid and SEO lets one agency move spend and organic visibility together, towards multi-day and away from Scotland day-tour volume.

### What the reassessment confirms

The original plan's two in-flight initiatives, Direct Packages 2.0 and navigation, rank first and second among the larger initiatives. The top-ranked workshop item, accommodation content on multi-day tour pages, is the top small project. The strongest quick win is the cheapest seat lever in the plan: selling the second tour straight after the first.

## The critical path

Five chains decide whether 2027 lands. A slip at the start of any of them moves its end.

1. **Profit visibility:** DC-02 load factor, contribution and commission → DC-03 Scotland target → SP-12 scorecard → every later priority call.
2. **Multi-day before January:** DC-06 cut scenarios → SP-04 accommodation content → SP-13 test → LI-02 Direct Packages rollout.
3. **Product data:** QW-03 time log and QW-04 locale answer → SP-02 attributes → LI-01 scope → DC-08 funding → LI-01 build → filters, availability, compare.
4. **Selling the second tour:** DC-07 finance decision → QW-15 cross-sell now → LI-06 basket later.
5. **Reachable customers:** QW-05 permission cause → SP-08 post-booking sequence with offers → SP-20 post-tour email → SP-22 rebook segmentation.

## How the plan is run

- **Monthly strategic review**, as the SOW provides, reports against the 2027 scorecard (SP-12), not against activity.
- **Quick wins are reported, not managed.** Small projects and larger initiatives carry tracker rows. Decisions carry an owner and a date.
- **Every action gets a measure and a baseline before it is scoped** (DC-11). The measures in this plan are proposed and need agreeing.
- **Cheap and reversible first.** Build only when volume justifies it.
- **Any change to the plan is written down**, with the reason, in the monthly review notes.
- **Sunny Lemons renewal.** The contract ends 05/01/2027. Liberandum and Sunny Lemons agree 2027 scope by 31/10/2026, with this plan as its basis and multi-day conversion and seats sold as the success measures. UX actions after 05/01/2027 depend on it. Monthly voice of customer synthesis continues under the SOW's package B.

**Not in scope, named rather than absorbed.** Research with non-bookers and basket abandoners is the largest evidence gap on the account and needs a separate commission. Copy is excluded from the Sunny Lemons SOW, so UX provides structure and content rules, and Content & Social writes the words.

# Private tours: outcomes, actions, backstage changes, impact and measurement

**Date.** 18/09/2026
**For.** Yourself, and the basis of anything taken to Liberandum or Rabbie's on private tours.
**Status.** Draft, v2. Synthesis of `research/2026-Q3-pvt/findings.md`, 6 private tour bookers,
Sep 2026, cross-checked against `analysis/sep-2026-rabbies-customer-insights-data.md`.

## Recommendation

Every outcome private tour customers want is produced backstage. Seven of the eight below need no
website change at all, and the one that does is a price and a link, not a build.

That determines who has to act. This is not a design backlog. It is a sequence change in how an
enquiry is handled, who is in the room when the trip is designed, and when accommodation gets
booked. None of it can be delivered by the agencies.

**No item below can be sized in money.** Private tour volume, revenue share and margin are not held
anywhere in the project, and there is no comparison between the margin on a private tour and the
margin on filled seats on a scheduled departure. Impact is stated as a mechanism and a direction.
Anyone converting these to a forecast is inventing it.

the next section. Ranking is the size of the mechanism multiplied by the confidence in it, and the

### Notes on the ranking

**Nothing in the measure column is instrumented today.** Items 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7 have no current
source of truth, and three of them depend on an enquiry to booking rate for private tours that is
not reported anywhere. That figure is the missing instrument for this entire programme and it has
not been asked for. Item 4 is the exception and is measurable within days with no build.

**Item 6 could be first and this evidence cannot tell.** Every participant converted, so anyone lost
to price opacity is structurally invisible. One participant scored the booking experience 9 rather
than 10 for this reason alone and another used the scheduled catalogue as a price anchor before
enquiring. If non-converting enquirer evidence ever arrives and names price opacity, item 6 moves to
the top.

**Item 8 is the gap between what customers ask for and what they act on.** It is the most requested
change in the research and it ranks last, because stated preference is not commercial effect and
four people asking for a call is not four people who would have left without one.

**Item 3 absorbed the qualification question**, which was a separate row in v1. Detection has to come
before any script: one customer wanted a trip created, one wanted his own corrected, one wanted
neither. A single improved opening serves one customer in three, so the triage is the change and the
script is only its output.

## Do order, which is different

Impact order above answers what is worth most. It does not answer what to start, because four of the
eight add consultant or driver hours to the pre-sale phase and are held against the margin question
in A16. The same distinction is made in `strategy/2026-09_rice-is-an-input-not-the-sequence.md`.

**Start now, no added hours, no build.** 4 deliverability and booking reference, 2 group needs record
and handoff, 6 indicative price, 7 survey trigger. Items 4 and 7 are also the two that make the rest
measurable, which is a second reason to start with them.

**Start now, process change, no added hours.** 1 accommodation sequence. Moving sourcing earlier
changes when work happens rather than how much of it there is. Worth confirming that with whoever
books the properties before assuming it.

**Held on A16.** 3 triage, 5 route knowledge, 8 planning call. Either wait for the margin comparison
or run them as a scoped trial on multi-day enquiries with hours recorded, so the cost becomes known
rather than estimated. The trial is the better option, because A16 may not be answered for months.

## A correction to the referral argument

The research implications name this as the strongest commercial argument in the study: one organiser
converts an entire party and becomes the acquisition route for the next one. The Spike customer data
does not support it as a general case. 89.3% of customers have booked once, roughly 3% of any year's
bookers return the following year, and where a second booking is a genuine return rather than the
same holiday split in two it is now worth 9.8% less than the first.

That does not kill the argument, and the two sources are measuring different things. Spike counts
individuals in a database dominated by day tour buyers. The referral claim is about a party leader in
a product that is a small fraction of that base, and R1's organisers may behave nothing like the
base rate. It does mean the claim cannot be asserted from a base rate that contradicts it, and it
cannot currently be tested either, because private bookings are not separable in the data.

Treat it as a hypothesis with a named test: whether organisers of private parties rebook at a
materially different rate from the 10.7% base. Do not put it in a deck as a reason to spend.

## What is deliberately not here

**A private tours page redesign.** 4 of 6 spent under ten minutes on site before making contact. Two
were walked through the current page live and neither found a question it failed to answer. The page
has two jobs, a price anchor and a route in from the scheduled catalogue, and both are item 6.

**An account system or itinerary hub.** One participant wants it, three are content with email and
PDFs, one advised against building it directly. The defensible version is item 4.

**A premium tier.** The purchase is delegation, not luxury. The one quote reading as a case for a
tier above was hedged by the participant in the same breath, and he attributed the feeling to the
channel rather than the product.

**Discounting.** 5 of 6 did not treat price as a barrier. The real price problem is legibility, which
is item 6.

## What this rests on

- **R1, the Appointed Organiser role**, in `personas.md`. These outcomes are written for someone
  carrying responsibility for a party they did not fully control. They do not describe the couple
  buying privacy, who is 1 participant in 6.
- **A16.** Growing private tour bookings is worth more than filling seats on scheduled departures.
  Rests on nothing. It is the premise underneath this entire memo and it gates four of the eight items.
- **A15.** Group-led demand is understated in the booking data because payments are split. If wrong,
  this segment is smaller than it looks.
- **A1.** The personas describe the market rather than only the people who bought.

## What would change this

All six participants converted. Nothing here explains why anyone enquired and did not book, so the
map is built on people the current service already won. If non-converting enquirer evidence arrives
and names a different blocker, item 6 moves to the top and the order below it is wrong.

The margin comparison coming back against private tours. That would not falsify a single outcome in
the table. It would mean fixing the service for the customers already buying it, and not growing the
volume.

# The 3 to 4 day duration gap

**Date.** 07/09/2026
**For.** Yourself.
**Status.** Draft. Options paper. This is Rabbie's product decision, not ours.

## Recommendation

Evidence the gap and hand it over. Do not carry it as a Sunny Lemons recommendation, because the deciding constraint is operational viability and that is not visible from here.

The specific ask: put it in front of a named owner on the Rabbie's side with the demand evidence attached and a request for a viability assessment. It currently has neither an owner nor an initiative, which for the largest claimed revenue opportunity on the account is the notable fact.

## Why

Repeatedly requested across research: 3 to 4 day options against 7 to 8 day tours perceived as too long. It appears in the Purposeful Adventurer persona and in two Segment D sessions independently. Prior research flagged it as the highest long-term revenue opportunity identified.

It also sits at the intersection of two things already established. Multi-day tours are trip anchors booked before flights, so duration determines whether the tour fits the trip at all rather than being a preference. And the Purposeful Adventurer arrives with a list written elsewhere, so a tour that does not fit the shape of their trip is not a near miss, it is a non-starter.

The reason to hand it over rather than recommend it: nothing on the website backlog depends on it, and the constraint is fleet, guides, accommodation partners and route economics. Sunny Lemons can evidence demand. It cannot assess whether a 3 to 4 day tour is viable at Rabbie's cost base.

## What this rests on

- A9: the duration gap is real unmet demand. Medium on the demand, low on viability. Viability has never been assessed.
- A1: the personas describe the market. Both the persona and the Segment D sessions come from the same booker-weighted evidence base.

## What would change this

Stated preference not converting. This is the classic stated-versus-revealed risk: people say they want a shorter tour and then book the seven-day one because it covers the places they came for.

Operational assessment showing a 3 to 4 day tour cannot be run at margin. That falsifies the recommendation without falsifying the demand, which is a distinction worth preserving when it comes back.

## Options considered and rejected

**Building it into the website backlog.** Rejected. There is nothing to build. The website can only sell what the catalogue contains.

**Recommending a specific product.** Rejected. Route design is not ours and proposing one invites a debate about the route instead of the gap.

**Testing demand through a landing page or waitlist before building.** Not rejected. This is the cheap reversible option and the strongest thing to offer alongside the handover: a notify-me or register-interest surface would produce revealed-preference data at very low cost. It overlaps the pre-released tours initiative, which is already scoped at 4 days and includes a notify-me component.

## Next

Name the owner. Then propose the waitlist test as the cheap first step, since it uses a component already scoped for another initiative and produces the evidence that stated preference cannot.
